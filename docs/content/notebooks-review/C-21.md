**3a. Cell 10, "### Step 3: drift in performance, once the true outcome arrives".** The reference error comes from a random split of hourly rows (`train_test_split(reference, test_size=0.3, …)`). The held-out hours sit between training hours, so the baseline is optimistic, and every error ratio, including the `ERROR_WATCH`/`ERROR_ALERT` comparison, is inflated by it. On this data a time-ordered holdout gives MAE 23.71 against 14.45. The text says only "part of the reference it did not train on", and the "does not show" paragraph is silent on this.

In the paragraph beginning "**What it does not show.** Why.", after "which is why you watch both.", add:
> The reference error here is measured on hours picked at random from the reference period, in among the hours the model trained on, so it is lower than the error on a later stretch of time the model has never seen, and every error ratio reads somewhat high for that reason.

**3b. Cell 14, "### Step 5: a fairness number per group, in each window".** "Or whether groups that are too small in a window have been left out" implies the cell leaves small groups out. Cell 15 does no such thing: `MIN_WINDOW_ROWS` skips whole windows, and every group in a judged window is reported, whatever its size, with no count shown.

Replace:
> **What it does not show.** Whether a gap is large enough to act on, or whether groups that are too small in a window have been left out. Small groups need the care shown in C-14.

> **What it does not show.** Whether a gap is large enough to act on. It also does not check how many rows each group has in a window: a group with few rows gives an unstable number, and this cell neither leaves it out nor warns about it. Small groups need the care shown in C-14.
