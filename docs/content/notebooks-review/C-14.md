In cell 16, replace item 3 (from `3. **Trend check.**` to `(this is Simpson's paradox).`) with:

### C14-03: change. Criterion 1. Cell 16 (`### Step 6: does the overall result hide`), checks 1 and 2

**Finding.** There are three problems.

1. Check 1 calls a group whose interval lies wholly below overall "below overall, and not by chance alone". The run compares 30 groups at 95 percent each, so one or two are expected outside by chance even if there is no real gap. The labels near the edge also depend on the seed. In `probe2.py`, a different seed changed the label of 2 groups (`[40-50) / Male` and `[50-60) / Male`) and another seed changed 1 group (`Female / AfricanAmerican`). With 2,000 resamples the same 8 groups came out below overall. "Not by chance alone" claims more than the method supports.
2. Cell 17 also writes an `AUC against overall` column into the per-group CSV, which the team is told to copy into the model card. No cell explains it, and check 2 explains why comparing a group's AUC with the overall AUC can mislead.
3. Check 2 says it reports "the lowest and highest group values", but the code uses only the reported groups of the first column in `GROUP_COLUMNS`.

**Exact change.**

In cell 16, replace item 1 with:

> 1. **Group against overall.** For each reported group it compares the group's recall with the overall recall. If the whole 95 percent interval is below the overall value, the group is **below overall**: for that one group, chance alone is an unlikely explanation. If the whole interval is above, it is above overall. Otherwise the data **cannot tell**. Many groups are checked at once (30 in the example), so one or two can land outside by chance even with no real gap. A group near the edge can also change label with another seed or number of resamples. The same comparison is made for AUC and written to the per-group file as `AUC against overall`; read that column with check 2 in mind.

In item 2, replace "The check reports the overall value next to the lowest and highest group values." with:

> The check reports the overall value next to the lowest and highest values among the reported groups of the first column in `GROUP_COLUMNS`.

### C14-07: change. Criterion 3. Cell 22 (`## What this does not show, and where to go next`)

**Finding.** The section is mostly honest. It says the notebook does not say the model is safe to use. It says groups that were not recorded, or were too small, are not examined, and that the summary gives how many. It says a gap is not a cause, and that labels can be biased.

Two points need fixing:
- "If it is not, the interval is too narrow" is not generally true. A bootstrap on a sample that does not represent the people you serve can be centred in the wrong place, and its width says nothing about that.
- The section does not mention the many-comparisons limit or the seed sensitivity that C14-03 describes. These are the main ways a reader could over-read the "below overall" list that the summary prints.

**Exact change.** In cell 22, replace the bullet beginning `- **Intervals are not guarantees.**` with these two bullets:

> - **Intervals are not guarantees.** A 95 percent interval from a bootstrap assumes the test set is a fair sample of the people you care about. If it is not, the interval can sit in the wrong place, and its width will not show it. With 200 resamples, the ends of an interval also move a little from one seed to another.
> - **Many groups are compared at once.** When many groups are each checked at 95 percent, one or two can fall below or above overall by chance even when there is no real gap. A single "below overall" among many is a reason to look closer, not a finding on its own.
