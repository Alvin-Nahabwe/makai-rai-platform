## Items

### 1a. Criterion 1 (methods in plain words): change

**Cell 8, "### Step 2: intersections, where groups get small".** "What this does" says it "reports how many cells are small or empty". `pd.crosstab` leaves out any combination of the row columns (here age band by sex) that nobody in the data has, so such a combination is counted neither as a cell nor as empty. In the example every age-band-by-sex row exists, so the printed numbers are right. On a team's own data with more or sparser columns, though, the most under-represented combinations (the ones with nobody at all) disappear from the count the text says it reports. The step has to say so.

Replace the cell's last paragraph:

> **What it does not show.** Which small cells matter. A small cell may be a group your use cares about a great deal.

with:

> **What it does not show.** Which small cells matter. A small cell may be a group your use cares about a great deal. It also does not show combinations of the row columns that nobody in the data has: such a combination is not a row of the table, so it is not counted among the cells or the empty ones. Check the row labels for combinations you expected to see.

### 1b. Criterion 1 (methods in plain words): change

**Cell 12, "### Step 4: comparison with reference shares you supply".** The code (cell 13) reindexes the observed counts to the groups named in `REFERENCE_SHARES`. People in any other group, including `missing`, are dropped without a word, and "data share" is a share of the people who remain. In the example the reference names every group, so nothing is dropped. For a team whose reference leaves a group out, the step would compare something different from what the text says ("compares the data's shares with the shares you gave") and give no sign of it.

Replace the first paragraph's last sentence:

> The example shares are **illustrative**: they stand for what a team would take from a census, a registry or a service's records.

with:

> The example shares are **illustrative**: they stand for what a team would take from a census, a registry or a service's records. Only the groups you name in `REFERENCE_SHARES` are compared: people in any other group, including missing values, are left out of this step, and the data shares are shares of the people who remain.
