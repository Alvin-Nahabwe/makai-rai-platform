### What a person will see

- **Changing someone else's answer opens who and when.** When the current answer names a decider other than the person changing it (another member, a former member, or an outside role), the form opens Decided by and Decided on, with the earlier decider chosen and the date empty, as in v0.9.0.
  - One press of Save is refused on the date ("Enter at least the year the decision was made.") until the person presses Today or types a date.
  - A first answer, an answer after a withdrawal, a change to your own answer, and a change of an answer that names no decider keep v0.10.0's one line, "Decided by you, <today> · Change".
  - The server is unchanged, by the owner's design: a decision a former member made before leaving can still be recorded, with its own date.
- **A records download's README says one more thing:** "A decision's reason is also empty when none was given, because a reason is optional. Only an erased reason has that separate entry."

**D-425 look-back (read only),** in the same shell, before the swap, while the host still runs v0.10.0, so no version saved under v0.10.1 can appear. It lists versions saved since 23 November that changed someone else's answer, keep the earlier version's decider, were saved by someone else, and declare, to the day, the day they were saved (give or take a day, for time zones). Those are the versions one press of Save could have made under v0.10.0. It prints ids and dates only, no names or answers, and runs inside a read-only transaction that is rolled back:

```bash
PGU=$(sed -n 's/^POSTGRES_USER=//p' ../.env)
$C exec -T postgres psql -U "$PGU" -d makrai -At -v ON_ERROR_STOP=1 <<'SQL'
BEGIN READ ONLY;
SELECT v."orgId", v."projectId", v."questionId", v."versionNumber", v."declaredOn", v."receivedAt"
FROM decision_versions v
JOIN decision_versions p
  ON p."orgId" = v."orgId" AND p."projectId" = v."projectId" AND p."questionId" = v."questionId"
 AND p."versionNumber" = v."baseVersionNumber"
WHERE v."receivedAt" >= '2026-11-23'
  AND v."withdrawalCode" IS NULL
  AND v."declaredPrecision" = 'day'
  AND (v."decidedByUserId" IS NOT NULL OR v."decidedByRole" IS NOT NULL)
  AND v."decidedByUserId" IS DISTINCT FROM v."enteredById"
  AND v."decidedByUserId" IS NOT DISTINCT FROM p."decidedByUserId"
  AND v."decidedByRole" IS NOT DISTINCT FROM p."decidedByRole"
  AND v."declaredOn" BETWEEN (v."receivedAt" AT TIME ZONE 'UTC')::date - 1 AND (v."receivedAt" AT TIME ZONE 'UTC')::date + 1
ORDER BY v."receivedAt";
ROLLBACK;
SQL
```

It prints `BEGIN`, one line per version found (if any), then `ROLLBACK`. A line is a version to look at, not proof of a wrong record: someone may really have recorded the earlier decider's decision on that day, and the window includes the hours of 23 November before v0.10.0 was deployed. It can miss two rare cases: a "Save mine" after a conflict, whose base is not the version it replaced, and a save retried more than a day after the date was filled in. What to do about any found is a separate decision; say how many there are in the session. The query was checked against the full migrated schema on a throwaway database (valid, 0 rows); it has not been run against real rows.
