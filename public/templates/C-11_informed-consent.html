C-11: Informed Consent Framework

Procedural Control — MAK-AI Responsible AI Toolkit

Grounding: Adapted from GDPR Articles 13-14 information requirements, WHO Ethics & Governance of AI for Health guidance on ongoing consent, and dynamic consent models from the Research Data Alliance. This template addresses AI-specific consent challenges not covered by standard research ethics forms.

1. Purpose & Data Practices

Per GDPR Art. 13: Clearly state what data you collect, why, and how it will be used. Use plain language accessible to non-experts.

2. AI-Specific Disclosures

These disclosures go beyond standard consent forms to address AI/ML-specific concerns. Per WHO guidance, participants should understand how AI will use their data.

⚠️ Critical: If the data will be used to train models that make decisions affecting individuals (e.g., credit scoring, clinical diagnosis), this must be clearly disclosed and participants must have the right to contest such decisions.

3. Participant Rights

Per GDPR Art. 15-22 and UNESCO AI Ethics Recommendation: Participants retain rights over their data even after consent.

4. Consent Verification

In LMIC contexts, consent must be meaningful — not just a signature on a form.

🌍 Global South consideration: Consider literacy barriers (support oral consent protocols), power imbalances (especially in healthcare/humanitarian contexts), community consent models (H3Africa Ethics guidelines), and language accessibility (provide consent materials in local languages).

5. Dynamic Consent & Updates

Per WHO guidance, consent should be an ongoing process, not a one-time event.

6. Review & Approval
